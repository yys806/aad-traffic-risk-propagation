## Question and method

The paper asks where a connected vehicle can positively influence a bottleneck macrostate. It uses an LWR model, a fixed freeway bottleneck, an event-triggered deceleration/acceleration policy and total vehicular delay as the macrostate. It defines a zone of influence (ZOI), an event horizon and a null horizon.

## Evidence and results

The paper derives analytical spatial limits and shows scenarios in which communication may need to span several kilometers. The result is a model-based action boundary for a specified bottleneck and control policy.

## What is actually shown

Spatially delimited influence of connected vehicles is already an explicit published concept. The paper does not provide randomized causal identification, message verification/adoption logs, or regional crash-risk mediation.

## Limitations and AAD relation

The macrostate is total delay, the bottleneck is activated once, and the admissible control is prescribed. This is a high-priority innovation threat: AAD must either generalize to risk and dual propagation with evidence, or narrow its claim.
